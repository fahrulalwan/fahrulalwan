# Case Study Content Requirements

Fill in each case study using this template. You have 4 stories ready — pick 2-3 strongest ones. Write raw, don't polish — we'll shape it together.

**NDA rules:** No company names, no project names, no client names. Use industry descriptors (e.g., "auction platform", "telco superapp", "fintech news portal").

---

## Template per Case Study

### 1. One-line headline
What did you do, in one sentence? Not a title — a statement.
- Good: "Took a legacy auction platform's real-time system from prototype to production — still running eight years later"
- Bad: "Legacy Platform Rebuild" (too generic, no stance)

### 2. Tags
3-5 tech/domain tags. e.g., `WebSocket`, `Angular`, `Real-time`, `Architecture`

### 3. Year
When did this happen?

### 4. Your role
What was your actual title AND what you actually did (these are often different).
- e.g., "Junior Frontend Developer — but I volunteered to architect the real-time communication layer"

### 5. Timeline & team size
How long, how many people.

### 6. Context (2-3 sentences)
What was the situation BEFORE you got involved? What was broken, missing, or needed?
- Write it like you're explaining to a friend, not a performance review.

### 7. The challenge (2-3 sentences)
What made this HARD? Not "we needed to build X" — that's a task, not a challenge.
- What constraints did you face?
- What made this non-obvious?

### 8. Key decisions (2-3 decisions)
For each decision:
- **What you decided** — one sentence
- **Why** — what was the trade-off? What did you consider and reject?
- **What happened** — did it work? Any surprises?

This is the MOST IMPORTANT section. CTOs care about HOW you think, not just WHAT you built.

### 9. Results
**Numbers** (be specific):
- Performance: load time, Lighthouse score, bundle size
- Business: delivery timeline vs estimate, adoption rate
- Team: onboarding time, developer satisfaction

**Qualitative:**
- What changed for the team, users, or business?
- Any unexpected positive outcomes?

### 10. Honest reflections (1-2)
What would you do differently? What did you learn?
- This is what separates your case studies from AI-generated ones. Be real.
